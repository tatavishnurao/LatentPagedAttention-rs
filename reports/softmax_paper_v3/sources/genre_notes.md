## 2. Patterns across the 12 papers

* **The abstract names the problem, the scope of what was checked, one to three concrete numbers, and the implication.** Measurement papers (2, 5, 7) put a headline number in the abstract. Reproduction papers (10, 11, 12) state whether the claims held.
* **Claims are numbered and tested one by one.** The MLRC/TMLR template (10, 11) has a "Scope of reproducibility" section listing claims, then a results subsection per claim. Paper 12 does the same informally.
* **A "pitfalls" or "anti-patterns" section.** Etalon §3.1 and paper 2 §4 show a specific metric or tool producing a misleading number, next to what it hides.
* **The tables people reuse:** a classification table of tools or approaches (2, 5); a workloads/SLO table (6, 9); an original vs reproduced table (10, 11); relative-improvement tables at several thresholds (7).
* **Metrics stated with explicit definitions,** often as a formula or a one-sentence definition (goodput in 9, fluidity-index in 1, NTPOT in 2). Tail percentiles (P90/P95/P99) are reported, not only means.
* **Validity is a section, not a footnote:** "Methodological reproducibility and constraints" (2), "Limitations" (2, 3), "What was difficult" (10), partial-test caveats (11).
* **Artifacts are linked from the abstract or an artifact section,** with configs archived (2, 3, 6).

## 3. Template we will follow (one page)

**Adopt from the MLRC/TMLR reproduction template (10, 11, 12):** a *Scope* section with numbered claims C1–C4, each with its source (paper, section, table); one results subsection per claim, each ending in a verdict (*holds*, *holds with conditions*, *does not hold in our setting*); a short "what was difficult" paragraph. Reason: reviewers of reproduction tracks expect it, and it forces each claim to be stated before it is tested.

**Adopt from the measurement papers (1, 2, 7, 9):**
* a methodology section that defines every metric precisely (sink mass, attention entropy, needle weight and fitted Δ, exact-zero rate of log p, collapse threshold);
* a short "pitfalls" table of places where a standard computation returns a misleading value (bf16 log-softmax → log p = 0; collapse thresholds that depend on the class count; adaptive temperature switching off at high entropy);
* tail-style reporting: distributions and per-layer curves, not only means;
* a workload/setup table: models, context lengths, dtypes, hardware.

**Adopt from the energy/benchmark papers (3, 6):** a threats-to-validity section, and an artifact section with commands, pinned versions and data files.

**Do not adopt:** proposing a new system or metric (1, 2, 3 do; this paper does not); headline improvement numbers. This paper's numbers are measurements of existing behaviour.

**Planned spine (4–6 pages):** Abstract (≤150 words) · 1 Introduction (contributions as reproduction and measurement) · 2 Claims checked (C1 dispersion and adaptive temperature, C2 sinks, C3 collapse, C4 cancellation) · 3 Methodology and metrics (Table: setups) · 4 Reproduction results, one subsection per claim, each with a verdict (Table: original vs reproduced) · 5 Real-model measurements (sinks, entropy, needle, log-prob precision) · 6 Implications for serving (Table: pitfalls) · 7 Threats to validity · 8 Artifact availability · References.
